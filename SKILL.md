## Panel-specific overlay

The transformed design is a large pale silhouette rubbed back and forth in crayon, edges dissolving, on warm-white paper, mostly blank, smaller than the page, clear of the horizontal fold. Subject, outline, structure, pose and story shrink to a few big shapes and one or two crooked lines; a scene keeps one object and one small thing. In the silhouette's lower half the subject is small and whole, with space above its head, while one small thing from the photograph is larger, filled solid, half and half in two colours. A circled light spot hangs above, a short thick dark stroke marks the silhouette's clearest detail, and the ground is a few crooked lines and grass tufts. People, animals, buildings and objects are left-handed doodles with big heads, small bodies, stick limbs and lines that wobble, break and ignore proportion. Crayon stays on the silhouette and the drawings only. Colour is warm white, a pale silhouette from the photograph's softest light, and a few saturated crayon colours, brightest only on the light spot and the enlarged object, with near-black lines and no dark silhouette. The only text is one short naive poem about this moment and something unseen, never naming what is drawn, in thin uneven near-black handwriting on staggered lines beside the silhouette, smaller than the subject, with no number, rule or label; Chinese is simplified. Reject a full-scene copy, a templated poster, skilled illustration, true proportions, a hard or dark silhouette, a filled page, crayon elsewhere, copied signs, extra captions, stock phrases, reflections, and titles naming the picture.

These overlay rules add to `references/soldier-runtime.md`. They never replace the source brief or the family runtime contract.
